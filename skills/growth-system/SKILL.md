# Growth System: what we apply

**Internal use only.** If we ever sell or share this, rebuild it first: own names, own structure, own words,
only claims our numbers back, credit to the source.

Distilled from a 7-day email course ("Short Form Growth System", same creator as the Triple Hook).
Only what is useful and applicable to The Exact Method is kept here; hype, income claims and
unverified stories are left out. Updated after every email (log at the bottom).

**How to use:** apply silently inside the normal audit (with ig-reel, content-principles and the
marketing skills). In the audit results, add one line per rule below that the piece passes or fails,
and break the piece into its 11 bricks (section 0).

---

## 0. Content is data + psychology: the 11 bricks

Data says which videos already worked; psychology predicts which will work again. Replace guessing
with a loop: study what works → build → measure → change one brick → repeat.

Every short video is a stack of **11 bricks in 5 categories**, decided in this order:

| Category | Bricks | Our sources |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Format | Format (the canvas: breakdown, scenario, hero's journey, result-first…) | `claude/viral-formats.md` (F1-F5) |
| Idea | Topic · **Seed** (the one-line premise) · **Substance** (facts, takes, examples that drive emotion) | relevance gate (section 3), real runs |
| Hook | **Text hook · Visual hook · Spoken hook**, in that order of importance | frame-0 rules, hookscore |
| Script | Story structure · CTA · retention | ig-reel beats, content-principles |
| Edit | Visual style, layout, pacing, captions, signature elements | Remotion system, no-slop-motion, real-sfx |

**In every audit:** write the 11 bricks out in one short table for the piece, and answer the 11 questions in
section 15 (one per brick). When a Reel
underperforms, diagnose by brick (which brick failed?), not by "the video was bad".

**Hook order matters:** the on-screen text hook is judged first, then the visual hook, then the
spoken hook. Score and rewrite the text hook (frame-0 headline) first; hookscore only rates the
spoken line, so it is the last check, not the main one.

**You don't need originality:** take brick combinations already working in the niche and remix them.
Keep most bricks, change about 3, add our own spin (real runs, the Exact Method angle). This is the
same idea as the viral-format match, applied brick by brick.

## 0b. The 4 "cannot lose" conditions

1. **Post consistently:** at least 3 a week, ideally 4-7. We post 4-5 a week. Keep it.
2. **Get measurably better each rep:** every Reel carries one **improvement hypothesis** (from day 6: one hypothesis per batch of 10 Reels, see section 15) ("testing a
   text hook that names a number", "switching the layout to X"). Write it in the audit and in the
   STATUS results log (Test column), then read it against the numbers.
3. **Funnel on-target traffic into an aligned offer:** the free guide must match what the Reel
   promised, and the guide must lead to the paid ladder. See sections 1 and 7.
4. **Never stop:** the core skills lock in around rep 50; most people quit around rep 25. We are at
   about 14 Instagram Reels (19 Nov 2026). Results lag skills early on; judge the system at rep 50.

## 1. Our game: Game 5, authority building

There are 5 content games (mainstream fame, category fame, intuitive products, explanatory products,
authority building). We sell info products ($0 free guides → $17 → $37 → $127), so we play **Game 5**:
build trust with people who could buy, then convert them off-platform (Gumroad).

- **Goal: maximum on-target views, not maximum views.** A view only counts if the viewer could
  become a buyer. 1,000 views from buyers beat 100,000 from the wrong audience.
- **Never chase virality for its own sake:** no broad entertainment, trends, skits or controversy
  that pull an audience who would never buy. Going viral with the wrong audience can make the next
  on-topic Reels perform worse.
- **No home runs needed.** Consistent, on-target, useful pieces plus a funnel that captures interest.

## 2. Who the buyer is (the on-target viewer)

People who **use AI to build, sell or create something**: beginners and small builders starting a
product, side business, content or freelance work, who want better results from ChatGPT/Claude/Gemini.
Not: people looking for AI news, AI for school homework, job seekers only, developers.

## 3. The relevance gate (before writing anything)

Ask: **"Would this viewer ever buy a $17–$127 product that teaches a method for getting reliable
results from AI?"** If no, change the topic before writing. Name the buyer the piece is for in the audit.

- Pass example: N-7 "Never sell ChatGPT's first idea" (what to sell first → builders).
- Fail example: W-6 resume prompt (job seekers, not builders).

## 4. Trust = minutes watched × relevance × usefulness

Treat it as a three-part check, not a score. A zero in any part makes the piece worthless.

| Part | Question | How we meet it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Minutes watched | Do they stay? | Hook and frame-0 rules, something new every 2-3 s, loop |
| Relevance | Is it about something a buyer cares about? | The relevance gate (section 3) |
| Usefulness | Can they apply it and get the promised result? | A real prompt or method from a real run, shown on screen, worth saving |

## 5. Hook breadth: broad within the buyer pool

The first words must stop **anyone in the buyer pool**, not everyone on Instagram, and not only a tiny
sub-niche. "What to sell first" stops builders; "your Claude video" (V-2) stopped only people already
making Claude videos; generic "ChatGPT is lying to you" style hooks pull everyone and convert no one.

## 6. Measure trust, not only views

Log per Reel in STATUS (with reach, skip rate, average watch):
**saves, shares, follows, keyword comments, free-guide downloads** (Gumroad). These are the Game 5
signals. Views without these mean the wrong audience or no usefulness.

## 7. Every piece feeds the funnel

Every piece ends in a capture step: comment keyword → DM → free guide on Gumroad (email captured).
Known gap: no nurture after the download until the Gumroad email workflows unlock ($100 earnings). Section 13 has the full funnel and the per-Reel conversion metric.
When writing those emails, use the email-course structure as the model (section 8).

## 8. Copy patterns worth copying (from how the emails are written)

- Open on a stake ("the most important decision you'll ever make").
- Name an enemy (a wrong approach the reader recognises), not a person.
- One specific personal number with an honest twist (e.g. "46M views, zero customers").
- A quick diagnostic so the reader places themselves.
- End with an open loop to the next piece ("tomorrow: …").
Use them in captions, DMs and the future email sequence. Our claims must stay true and checkable.

## 9. Testing discipline

Change one thing per Reel, log it in STATUS, read it against the baseline. Treat new formulas from
the course as tests, not rules, until our numbers confirm them.

## 10. Ideas: where they come from and how to pick them (day 2)

An idea = **Topic + Seed + Substance**. A video outperforms when two things are true:
(1) the **topic** is relevant to our buyer (section 3), and (2) the **seed and substance** are
genuinely interesting or valuable to that buyer. "Lots of people like this topic" is not a reason:
views from the wrong audience don't become sales.

**The 6 idea pipes, ranked by hit rate.** Use them in this order:

| # | Pipe | What it means for us |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Our own outliers | Remake our best Reels with a new seed or substance. Judge "best" by the Game 5 signals (avg watch, saves, keyword comments), not views alone. Today: N-7 (8.0 s avg watch, best so far) |
| 2 | Competitor short-form outliers | **The main source: 80%+ of research time.** Reels that beat their own account's normal views, in our niche. Vet each for buyer relevance before remaking |
| 3 | Outliers in other media | Long YouTube videos, podcasts, newsletters, X posts with proven interest, translated into a Reel |
| 4 | Category news | New AI features and releases. No proof of interest, but can be big. Only when it serves the buyer, not AI-news viewers |
| 5 | Competitor non-outliers | Good ideas that were badly executed. Riskier |
| 6 | Originals | Our own ideas with no data. Highest risk, highest reward. Keep these rare until the eye is trained |

**Idea Mining, every 2 weeks** (our free version of his paid-tool process, run with the ig-viral skill):
1. **Watchlist:** 10-15 accounts serving our buyer (people using AI to build, sell or create), 10K-1M
   followers, on Instagram, TikTok or YouTube Shorts. The same list doubles as the engagement-round list.
2. **Outliers:** their Reels from the last 30 days, ranked by outlier score = views ÷ that account's
   median views over the 90 days before the post (3x = three times normal). A useful floor for "worth a
   look": outlier score ≥ 1, ≥ 25,000 views, engagement ≥ 2% (engagement = (likes + comments) ÷ views).
3. **Topics:** scan the top 50-100, write down the 5-7 topics that keep winning. Those are the focus areas.
4. **Seeds:** for each winner in those topics, write its one-line non-obvious takeaway. Then ask: do we
   have something more interesting, different or better (usually a real run)? If yes, that is our new
   seed on a proven topic. If no, or if it's off-topic for our buyer, skip it.
5. **Shortlist:** 10-20 ideas per batch in one running doc: topic + new seed + link to the original
   outlier. Build Reels from this list, not from blank-page brainstorming.

**Analyse each outlier on the shortlist with 5 questions** (from his tool's idea analysis):
- Positioning: what is the unique angle?
- Format: which storytelling format, and why does it work here? (match to F1-F5)
- Contrast: what common belief does it flip, and what is the contrarian reality?
- Hook alignment: does the hook promise exactly what the video delivers?
- Personalise: 3 ways to adapt it for our buyer and our method.

**Remix rule:** keep 8-10 of the 11 bricks that worked, change 1-3 (usually topic, seed, substance).
The more bricks you keep, the higher the chance it works. Inside our niche, the topic often carries over;
outside it, borrow only the format, hook, script or visuals.

Example: "the 3 levels of toothpaste" → a dental surgeon makes "the 3 levels of veneers" with the same
format, hook words, script and layout. Ours: a winning "3 levels of X" Reel → "3 levels of a ChatGPT prompt".

## 11. Script structure (Short-Form Storytelling guide)

Every script is built from these parts, in this order:

| Part | Length | Job |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hook | 1-2 sentences | Name the subject and open curiosity |
| Lock-in zone | 2-4 sentences | Convince them the rest is worth it ("I have to keep listening") |
| Body block 1 | 3-5 sentences | One non-obvious point + 2-4 sentences of proof (facts, example, reason) |
| Rehook | 1 sentence | Reset attention: contrast with block 1 and open a new loop |
| Body block 2 | 3-5 sentences | A second non-obvious point + proof |
| (Rehook 2 + body block 3) | optional | Only if the length needs it |
| Closing | 1-2 sentences | Optional; skip it when there's a CTA (we always have one) |
| CTA | 1 sentence | A value ramp tied to the topic: our free guide (lead magnet) |

**Hook = the 4 S's:** Subject named in the first sentence · Stakes (why the buyer must keep watching) ·
Simple words · Superclear, punchy sentence structure. Use it alongside the frame-0 rules and hookscore.

**Lock-in zone, 6 ways** (pick one per Reel and name it in the audit):
teasing proof (a real result) · teasing a transformation or feeling · clarifying contrast (why this is
different from the common belief) · a "magic box" (a named method or term they want to know, e.g.
"The Narrowing Method") · objection handling · doubling down on the hook's claim.
Our default is **teasing proof**: the real run's result on screen by second 5.

**Two hits of value:** aim for at least two body blocks, each with its own non-obvious point. Two
"huh, I didn't know that" moments make people follow at a much higher rate than one.

**Rehooks:** viewers have about 20-25 s of attention even when interested; one rehook between the
blocks buys another stretch. Make it a real contrast ("…but that's not even the strange part"), never a
corny filler line. At 45-90 s: one rehook between body 1 and 2, a second before body 3 if used.

**Principles:** cut all fluff; as short as possible (don't add length unless needed); every sentence
either continues the last one or clearly starts a new thread; tease the value as early as possible;
simplest words; clarity over everything.

**Length: 45-90 s** (his ideal). Our 15-25 s test (18-20 Nov) didn't beat it, so from 23 Nov every Reel
runs 45-90 s, with two body blocks and a rehook; 45 s is the floor, never padding.

Example, P-6 mapped: hook "Never send ChatGPT's first draft. This one has nine giveaways." · lock-in
(teasing proof) "This one has zero. Same request, same ChatGPT." · body 1: the nine marks · rehook
"My plain-writing rules alone still left seven." · body 2: the one line that took it to zero · CTA.

## 12. Hooks: alignment, clarity, contrast (day 3)

If a viewer leaves at second 4, the rest might as well be a black screen. Short-form has no thumbnail,
title or description: the hook is all of them. **Spend 30-40% of a Reel's creation time on the hook**
(text, visual and spoken), not 5%.

**Order of importance** (as in section 0): text hook > visual hook > spoken hook. About 85% of short
videos are watched without sound, so the text and the picture must work on mute. A good spoken line can't
save weak text and visuals.

**Max alignment:** all three hook parts must say the *same thing*. Any split second of "wait, what is
this?" makes people leave, even when the content is good. His two examples, same creator and quality,
350x apart in views:
- 14M views, aligned: spoken "These are called Lifesize Floor Plans. You can walk through your home before
  you build it." · visual: people walking through a full-size floor plan · text: "LIFESIZE PLANS".
- 40K views, misaligned: spoken "This company is sending shipping containers into space" · visual: a cartoon
  of a brand name, then a pipe in a workshop · text: "LONGSHOT" (a brand name nobody knows).
Check in every audit: write the text hook, the frame-0 visual and the spoken hook in one line each. Do all
three point at one idea a stranger understands in 1 s? A name the viewer doesn't know yet (product, method,
term) is not a text hook on its own.

**Every hook needs two things:**
1. **On-target clarity:** after the first sentence the viewer knows what it's about and whether it's for them.
   Simple words (about a 10-year-old's reading level), short direct sentences.
2. **A curiosity loop = contrast:** the gap between what the viewer believes now and what you're about to
   show. The bigger the gap, the stronger the hook. His example: "stop putting money in your 401K; open a
   [new account type] instead". Ours: "Never sell ChatGPT's first idea. Its third one costs €0." (belief:
   the first answer is fine → reality: the third is better). Clarity gets them in; contrast keeps them.

**Finding hooks:** don't invent from scratch. In the idea-mining pass (section 10), note each outlier's
text, visual and spoken hook, and remix the structure for our topic. Keep a running list of hook
structures that won in our niche.

**Layout seen in both his example Reels (worth testing):** split screen. Top half: the thing itself (product
footage, real result); bottom half: the presenter talking; one short caption line in yellow at the seam.
Our version: the real AI result on top, Sage talking below.

## 13. The funnel: views → emails → sales (day 4)

**The goal of a Reel is trust and an email address, not a sale.** Selling happens later, in email.
Judge Reels by **conversions per view**: how many free-guide signups (emails) each Reel drives. A Reel with
5K views and 50 signups beats one with 50K views and 5. Make more of what drives signups.

**The Buyer Activation Flow (his funnel, ours mapped):**
Reel → comment KEYWORD → auto-DM → on-target free resource → page with one email field → delivery →
email sequence → offer.
Ours: Reel → comment NARROW/PLAIN/VIDEO → DM (scheduled task, later OpenReply) → Gumroad $0 product
(email captured at checkout) → PDF → **email sequence: missing** (Gumroad workflows drafted, locked until
$100 earnings) → $17 / $37 / $127.

**7 lead magnet types** (weakest to strongest fit for us): newsletter signup page; single doc download
(easiest, start here; = our free PDFs); email course (drip); free community; waitlist form; free trial /
account page; sales page. A keyword that sends people straight to a sales page skips the email step.

**Which lead magnet:** the closer it is to the Reel's content, the higher the uptake
("content ↔ audience ↔ lead magnet fit"). Three options: a light version of the paid product, a deep doc
on one painful step (ideally the one the Reel covered), or the written-up version of the Reel itself
(saves them taking notes). Aim for 3-5 anchor lead magnets, rotated by Reel topic (we have 3).

**The page (30-45 min max, never over-engineer):** headline = the outcome they want; subheadline = what
they get, specifically; 3 bullets of what's inside; one field (email); button "Send me the [thing]".
A plain page that exists beats a beautiful one that doesn't.

**Log per Reel (STATUS):** keyword DMs sent and free-guide downloads in the 48 h after posting, next to
reach. Downloads per 1,000 reach is the number to compare Reels by.

## 14. First 3 Reps: remix one outlier per video (day 5)

The rule: stop preparing, make reps. Each of the next videos is a close remix of one proven outlier.

1. **Pick:** 3 outliers at **5x+** the creator's normal views (stricter than the 3x mining floor), from accounts
   1-2 stages ahead (10K-1M followers), in **3 different formats**. Choose the formats that are cheapest for us
   to make (we're faceless: Sage, screenshots, screen recordings, animated lists).
2. **Study:** the hook in all three layers (text, visual, spoken) and the script beat by beat. Get the transcript
   (for us: a screen recording from the user + faster-whisper, or the outlier's caption if it carries the script).
3. **Remix: hold 4 constant, change 1.** Keep format, hook structure, script flow and visual style; change the
   idea (our angle, our real run, our examples). Ways to change it: a different/better set of items, fewer items,
   the same items framed for our buyer, or the same items with better examples.
4. **Script from the transcript as a fill-in template:** match it beat for beat (hook, setup, payoff, CTA), swap
   in our topic and run, read it aloud, rewrite anything unnatural.
5. **Production stays cheap:** match the outlier's pacing and look; captions on. The lead magnet and its delivery
   must be live **before** posting (for us: the keyword is in the DM task / bot, the freebie link works).
6. **The point is data:** the first 3 won't be great; they exist so the next audit (day 6) has something to read.

How we apply it: in each Reel's audit, name the outlier it remixes (account, link, multiple), list the 4 bricks
kept and the 1 changed. This sits on top of the viral-format match (F1-F5); remixing a specific 5x+ outlier
comes first when one fits. Candidates from batch 3: mrcreates.ai 115.7x / adilet.fndr 33.6x (Reels made with
Claude → ideas 19-20), murphmaxxing 15.7x (big model plans, cheap model builds → idea 24), techguyver's
"I reverse engineered ManyChat" (→ our DM-bot build, once live).

## 15. The 11 questions, batch analysis, triple down (day 6)

**The 11-question check (one per brick).** After every Reel (and before building, as part of the audit), answer each
honestly. "Would I watch this?" If not, why would they.

| # | Brick | The question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Format | Is this format the most compelling frame for this information or story? |
| 2 | Topic | Do we have data that our buyer cares about this (an outlier, comments, searches), or is it only interesting to us? |
| 3 | Seed | Is the angle non-obvious to the buyer, and does it challenge something they believe in a useful way? |
| 4 | Substance | Can they do something right after watching that moves them toward their result? Or is it only entertainment? |
| 5 | Visual hook | Does frame 0 stop the scroll: visual contrast, movement, something unexpected? |
| 6 | Text hook | Does the on-screen text make them need to know more, and does it match the visual and spoken hooks? |
| 7 | Spoken hook | After the first 3 s, is the subject 100% clear AND is there a curiosity loop? |
| 8 | Story structure | Does it build anticipation, or just list facts? |
| 9 | CTA | Is there one clear next step for interested viewers (comment KEYWORD → DM → freebie)? |
| 10 | Visual layout | Does the layout play to our strengths? (We're faceless: Sage, real screens, animated lists. Don't force formats that need a face.) |
| 11 | Elements | Do cuts, captions, sound and effects match the pace the content needs (fast = fast cuts, animated captions; slow = longer holds, minimal captions), and is it always clear what's happening? |

These are the same 11 bricks as section 0, phrased as pass/fail questions. In `scripts/reel-audit.md` they are
section H; the detailed checks A-G stay underneath them.

**Batch analysis: every 10 Reels, not per Reel.** Single-Reel numbers in the first day mislead (some Reels
break out after days). Log every Reel's numbers in STATUS as usual, but only draw conclusions per batch of 10:
1. **Pull data (5 min):** views, follows gained, and conversions (keyword comments → DMs → guide downloads /
   emails, from the bot log `/log`, Gumroad and Kit) for all 10. Rank best to worst as a cohort.
2. **Find outliers (15 min):** any Reel at 5x+ the batch average on views, follows or conversions. Run the 11
   questions on each winner and the worst losers. What did winners have that losers didn't?
3. **Write hypotheses (10 min):** e.g. "text hooks are weak: winners had a curiosity gap, losers didn't", "bottom 5
   had topics without demand data", "layout inconsistent: we need one signature look".
4. **Test ONE change for the next 10 Reels.** One brick only, held for all 10 reps, then measure again.

Our batches: Reels are numbered in STATUS; batch 1 = the 10 Reels from W-7 on (23 Nov). The per-Reel improvement
hypothesis (section 0b) becomes one hypothesis per batch.

**Triple down.** When one of our own Reels hits 10x+ our average (views, follows or conversions), immediately
make 3-5 more just like it: same format, hook structure and visual style; change only the seed or substance. If
a second one also hits, that combination is a locked formula: run it until it stops working, then go back to
experimenting. Goal: 2-3 locked formulas. Our own outliers are pipe 1 in section 10 and always beat competitor
remixes.

## 16. The road from first video to lead engine (day 7)

Expectations, so we don't quit early (quitting comes from expecting results the phase can't give):

| Phase | Reps (at 4-5 a week) | Goal | Target per Reel |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Skill development | months 1-3, ~50 Reels | close the skill gaps, get clean data | 5-10K **qualified** views by rep 50; any outlier is a bonus |
| 2. Systems | months 4-6, ~50 more | lock 2-3 winning formulas, test in batches | 10-20K qualified views |
| 3. Compounding | months 7-12, ~100 more | scale output, refine the funnel | 25-50K+ qualified views |

- **Volume is the clock, not time.** 3 a week turns 6 months into 10; 2 a week into 16. Keep 5 a week (Mon-Fri).
- **The danger zone is around rep 25**, right before the curve starts. Don't change the plan there because of flat
  numbers; read the batch (section 15) and fix one brick.
- **Feedback speed is the real advantage.** The 11 questions tell us what to look at; to learn which brick to fix
  on OUR video, compare each batch against our own baseline and against the outliers we remixed, and say which
  brick lost (topic, hook execution at 0:00-0:04, format...). Our stand-in for a coach: the batch analysis plus an
  outside review (fresh-eyes audit) of the weakest Reel each batch.
- Ideas and Hooks were the only two bricks this course covered in depth; Format, Story Structure, Visual Layout and
  CTA get the 11-question check only.
- Where we stand (25 Nov): about 25-30 Reels in, Phase 1. Measure "qualified views" = views from people who use AI
  to build, sell or create (not raw reach).

The rest of the email is the Short Form Academy pitch ($1,297 a month, 2 feedback calls a week); not adopted.
