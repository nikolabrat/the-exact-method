The habits readers notice.

One habit means little. Five in one paragraph is what makes text read as AI-written. The rules start from Wikipedia's "Signs of AI writing" list and keep the ones that show up in emails, posts and essays.

Importance inflation

"marks a pivotal moment", "a testament to"

Bans the phrases, asks for the plain fact

The contrast formula

"It's not just a tool, it's a way of working."

Bans the "not just X, but Y" pattern

Formatting overload

Headings, bullets and bold on a three-line answer

Plain paragraphs unless you ask

Recaps and closers

"In conclusion...", "Let me know if you'd like..."

No recaps, no closing offers

None of these habits proves a text was written by AI, and removing them won't make weak writing strong. It takes away the most obvious signals.

Before you download.

Will this make my text pass AI detectors?

No, and the guide doesn't promise that. Detectors disagree with each other and misfire on human writing. The rules remove the patterns people notice.

Which tools does it cover?

ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini, with a separate version for each. Don't mix lines between them.

Does it check facts?

No. It changes how the AI writes. What it knows stays the same, so check every fact, number and source before you publish or send anything.

Can I change the rules?

Yes. The five-minute check shows which lines make a difference. Delete the ones that don't.
