### What wins on Pinterest (and how it differs from Instagram)

1. **The save is the goal, and people save dense reference pages.** 12 of the top 16 are a one-page cheat sheet
   or a carousel of many prompts. Pinterest users save to use later, so more on the page means more reason to
   save. **This contradicts our pin standard (6)** ("at most 3 short evidence items, no mini-document panels, one
   idea per pin"), which came from a ChatGPT stress test, not from data. → decision for the user below.
2. **A big number in the title:** 59, 99, 100, 120 prompts / codes / products. 29 of the top 60 have a number.
3. **"Secret codes" / "cheat sheet" framing** on 5 of the top 16. It clashes with our calibrated-claims rule
   (no hype); "cheat sheet" is the honest version of the same promise.
4. **Carousels over-index:** 2% of all pins but 8 of the top 60 (13%). Each slide = one prompt, so "one idea per
   slide" still holds.
5. **The Plain Voice topic is proven here** (unlike on Instagram, where rubenhassid's "write like a human" Reel
   flopped): Eman_Knows' "Stop telling ChatGPT to 'write like a human'" = 10,049 saves.
6. **A real chat screenshot with the full prompt** (detrarogers 14.9K) matches our "real run" style exactly.

### Decision needed (user)

Our pin standard (6) says one idea per pin, at most 3 items, no mini-document panels. The data says dense
cheat sheets and carousels get the saves. Options: (a) keep the rule, (b) allow one dense "cheat sheet" pin
type next to the current ones and compare saves after 3 weeks, (c) switch fully. Recommended: (b).
