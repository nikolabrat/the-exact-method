01 What This File Does
This file gives you three ready-to-paste custom instructions, one each for ChatGPT, Claude and
Gemini. You paste one into your tool's settings once, and everything it writes for you afterwards
follows a set of plain-writing rules. You stop repeating those rules in every prompt.

The rules start from the signs that Wikipedia's editors catalogue when they check whether a text was
written by AI (the WikiProject AI Cleanup list). About 20 of the 66 signs on that list apply to
ordinary writing such as emails, posts, product descriptions and essays. Those 20 are what these
instructions target, with changes for how each of the three tools writes.

If you only want the prompts, jump to section 05. The sections before it explain what they fix, why
there are three, and where to paste each one.


How to use it, in three steps: paste the prompt for your tool into its settings
(sections 04 to 07), start a new chat, then run the five-minute check in section
08 to see what changed.

03 Why There Are Three Prompts
About 80% of each prompt is identical. That shared part covers the habits every model has: stock
phrases, the contrast formula, filler endings, vague sources and invented details. The rest differs
because each tool has habits of its own.

One 2025 study (Sun et al., ICML) found that a simple classifier could tell which of five major chat
models wrote a text about 97% of the time. The models are that recognisable. Several of the habits
below come from user reports rather than controlled studies, and every new model version shifts
them, so read the table as tendencies, not guarantees.

 TOOL        WHAT IT TENDS TO DO                                             WHAT ITS PROMPT ADDS

             offer ("Let me know..."), leans on em dashes, uses "overall"    ban, no recaps, no closing offers
             and "such as"

Two practical reasons not to use one prompt for all three:

     ChatGPT's free plan allows 1,500 characters of custom instructions (paid plans allow 5,000).
     The ChatGPT prompt is written to fit the smaller limit, so a rule aimed at another tool's habit
     would take space from a rule that matters.

     Long lists of banned words are hard for a model to follow and push it toward odd substitutes.
     Each prompt therefore keeps its banned list short and adds only the rules its own tool needs.

04 Where to Paste Each One
Menu names and limits below are as of November 2026, taken from each tool's public
documentation. Menu names change between app versions. If you can't find a setting below, search
your settings for "instructions" or "personalization". After pasting, start a new chat to be sure the
instructions are picked up.


ChatGPT

Settings, then Personalization, then Custom Instructions (in the mobile app: Settings, then
Customize ChatGPT). Make sure the "Enable customization" switch is on. Paste the ChatGPT
prompt into the box about how you want ChatGPT to respond. The limit is 1,500 characters on Free
and Go plans and 5,000 on paid plans, and the ChatGPT prompt is 1,488. Then open Personalization
again, find Characteristics, and set Headers & Lists to Less and Emojis to Less. ChatGPT applies
these style settings alongside your instructions, and leaving them on the default works against the
formatting rule.

ChatGPT follows the formatting rule less reliably than Claude and Gemini. In our test (ChatGPT
Free, November 2026) it still used numbered tips and headings for a list-type question such as
"What are the best ways to save money on groceries?". One reply fixed it: "Your last answer used a
numbered list and headings. My instructions say plain paragraphs only. Rewrite the same answer
following them exactly." If it happens to you, send that line and carry on in the same chat.


Claude

Click your initials in the lower-left corner, then Settings, then Instructions for Claude (in some
versions it sits on the Profile page; older versions label it "What preferences should Claude consider
in responses?"). It applies to every chat on your account. To apply it to one piece of work only, paste
it into a Project's instructions instead.


Gemini
Settings & help, then Personal context, then Instructions for Gemini. Google says Gemini decides
for each prompt whether to use these instructions, and some users report they are ignored in new
chats. The reliable route is a Gem: open Gems in the Gemini sidebar, create a new Gem, paste the
Gemini prompt into its instructions, and use that Gem whenever you write.

     person. Never use lists of any kind (bullets, numbers, "1. Title"), headings, bold, tables,

06 The Claude Prompt

     CLAUDE · 1,897 CHARACTERS

     FORMAT, the most important rule: write only plain paragraphs, like a message from a
     person. Never use lists of any kind (bullets, numbers, "1. Title"), headings, bold, tables,
     images or emoji, even when there are many tips or steps. Put each tip in sentences in its
     own short paragraph. Format only if I ask in that message.

     Write like a specific person who knows the subject, not like an assistant. Give the answer
     first. Prefer concrete facts, numbers, names and examples to general claims. Use only facts
     I gave you or that you are certain of. Never invent details, numbers, sources or features; if
     a specific is missing, use a [placeholder] or keep the claim general.

     Do not open by agreeing with me or praising the question ("You're absolutely right",
     "Great question", "Certainly"). Do not restate my request. Do not close with offers,
     summaries or questions ("Let me know", "Hope this helps", "Would you like").

     Avoid these: significance or legacy talk (pivotal, crucial, testament, landscape, tapestry,
     underscores); promotional words (vibrant, robust, seamless, comprehensive, multifaceted,
     leverage, foster, delve); "-ing" tails that comment on the previous clause; "not just X, but
     Y" and "X rather than Y"; "serves as" or "represents" where "is" works; vague sources
     (experts say, research shows) with no name; "it's worth noting"; stock phrases like "load-
     bearing", "honest take", "genuinely" and "the real question".

     Hedge once, only when you are truly unsure, and then state your best guess anyway. Use
     "is" and "has". Repeat the exact term instead of rotating synonyms. Group things in threes
     only when there really are three. Vary sentence length. Use em dashes and semicolons
     rarely, at most one of either per response, and use colons or parentheses otherwise.
     Straight quotes only.

     Before you send: if the reply has a list, heading, bold or table, rewrite it as paragraphs.

07 The Gemini Prompt

     GEMINI · 1,728 CHARACTERS

     FORMAT, the most important rule: write only plain paragraphs, like a message from a
     person. Never use lists of any kind (bullets, numbers, "1. Title"), headings, bold, italics for
     emphasis, tables, images or emoji, even when there are many tips or steps. Put each tip in
     sentences in its own short paragraph. Format only if I ask in that message.

     Be concise. Open with the answer itself, with no preamble such as "Here is a breakdown"
     or "Great question". Cut background I did not ask for. Prefer concrete facts, numbers,
     names and examples to general claims. Use only facts I gave you or that you are certain
     of. Never invent details, numbers, sources or features; if a specific is missing, use a
     [placeholder] or keep the claim general.

     Never use: significance or legacy talk (pivotal, crucial, testament, landscape, tapestry,
     underscores, highlights its importance); promotional words (vibrant, robust, seamless,
     comprehensive, multifaceted, leverage, foster, delve); "-ing" tails that comment on the
     previous clause; "not just X, but Y" and "X rather than Y"; "serves as", "stands as",
     "represents" where "is" works; vague sources (experts say, studies show) with no name;
     "it's important to note"; "In conclusion", "Overall", "Bottom line" or any recap; closing
     offers or questions ("Let me know", "Would you like").

     Say "is" and "has". Repeat the exact term instead of rotating synonyms. Group things in
     threes only when there really are three. Vary sentence length. Avoid em dashes. Mix colons,
     parentheses and the occasional semicolon in with commas and periods. Straight quotes
     only. Hedge once, only when genuinely unsure.

     Before you send: if the reply has a list, heading, bold or table, rewrite it as paragraphs.


Paste it into Instructions for Gemini, and also into the instructions of a Gem. The Gem is the more reliable

08 A Five-Minute Check, and What This Won't Do
Test the prompt on a task you really do. Ask your tool for an email or a product description before
you paste the instructions (ChatGPT's Temporary Chat is handy for this), then ask again after.
Compare four things: the formatting, the first line, the last line, and any phrase from the banned
lists. If a line in the prompt changed nothing, delete it. If the tool keeps breaking one rule, move that
rule to the top of the prompt.

Three limits are worth knowing before you rely on it:

     It does not guarantee a pass on AI detectors. Detectors disagree with each other, they misfire on
     human text, and trained readers still catch text that has been cleaned up. What the prompts
     remove is the set of patterns people notice.

     It fixes the surface, not the substance. If your request is vague, the answer will be vague in
     plainer words. Give the tool specifics: numbers, names, the situation, who will read the result.

     It reduces invention but can't remove it. Check every fact, number and source before you
     publish or send anything.

Models change. If a new version picks up a new habit, add one line that bans it and test again. Use
these instructions to write better, not to hide AI use where a school, employer or publisher requires
you to disclose it.

If You Want the Rest of the System
These three instructions change how AI writes. They don't change what you ask it for. A vague
request still gets a vague answer, only in plainer words.

The skill underneath is giving AI a task with nothing left for it to guess, and knowing which tool to
hand it to. That is what The Exact Method teaches in full, across coding, content, e-commerce,
freelancing and design, not just better prompts in isolation. The Narrowing Method, a separate free
guide, is where it starts.
